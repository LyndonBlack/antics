Antics Revisited is a free, open-source rebuild of the engine behind Spy vs. Spy III: Arctic Antics, the 1987 DOS game: two spies, one iceberg, three rocket parts, a blizzard coming in and a lot of snowballs.

It is written from scratch in Rust and runs on Windows, macOS and Linux. It draws every screen itself with a port of the original's own drawing code, run on your own copy of the game. Nothing from the original is in this project, so you need to bring the game files.

Yes this is Vibecoded. However there was around 100 hundred hours of human involvement during initial game recording, tool creation, port testing and banging of head against wall. Should have just used DosBox! This one paragraph was written by hand.

Every release has a SHA256SUMS file so you can check what you downloaded. The programs are not code-signed, so Windows and macOS will ask you to confirm once (see below). All releases: github.com/LyndonBlack/antics/releases.

You need the original game

antics contains no program code, graphics or sound from Spy vs. Spy III: Arctic Antics. To play, you supply the original game's files from a copy you are entitled to use. Nine of them are needed: SPY.EXE, SPYTITL.EXE, TITLE.DAT, TRAP.PIC, MAXX.PIC, DEMO.DAT and TEXT1.DAT, TEXT2.DAT, TEXT3.DAT (the 256-colour CGA release). antics checks each one's size and SHA-256 and tells you plainly if something is not the supported release.

Sizes, checksums and details are in YOU-NEED-THE-ORIGINAL.md. Where you get your copy, and whether you may use it, is your responsibility; the game is still under copyright.

Set-up

Windows

  1. Download and run the setup program. Windows may say “Windows protected your PC” because the installer is not code-signed: choose More info, then Run anyway.
  2. Pick where to install antics (the default is fine; no administrator rights are needed).
  3. Pick the folder that holds your copy of the game, the one with SPY.EXE in it. You can leave it empty and do it later.
  4. Finish, and start antics from the Start Menu.

Prefer the zip? Unpack it, open a Command Prompt in that folder and run antics.exe --install C:\path\to\your\game, then antics.exe.

macOS

  1. Download the build for your Mac and double-click it to unpack.
  2. Open Terminal, go to the unpacked folder, and tell macOS you trust it (first time only): 
    cd ~/Downloads/antics-*-macos-*/
xattr -d com.apple.quarantine ./antics
  3. Point it at your game folder once: 
    ./antics --install ~/path/to/your/game
  4. Play: ./antics

Or right-click antics and choose Open instead of the xattr step.

Linux

  1. Unpack the archive and go into the folder: 
    tar xzf antics-*-linux-x86_64.tar.gz
cd antics-*-linux-x86_64/
  2. Point it at your game folder once: 
    ./antics --install ~/path/to/your/game
  3. Play: ./antics

SDL2 is built in, so there is nothing else to install. Run it from the folder you unpacked it in, or move antics to ~/.local/bin to run it from anywhere.

Your game files are copied to a per-user folder (%APPDATA%\antics, ~/Library/Application Support/antics or ~/.local/share/antics). Your original folder is never changed. Run antics --where (./antics --where on macOS and Linux) to see the path. Add --verbose to see what it is doing, and --no-sound to mute it.

How to play

Each spy needs the three parts of the escape rocket: the gyroscope, the fuel tank and the punch card. Find them around the iceberg (the map shows where), put them in your briefcase, and get to the rocket hatch before the clock runs out. Meanwhile your body heat is draining: keep out of deep water, warm up in an igloo, and make life difficult for the other spy with the Trapulator: saws that cut holes in the ice, buckets of water that make ice patches, buried dynamite, and snowballs.

The set-up screen

Use the arrow keys to move the highlight ring and Return to change the option under it: the island, whether the launch site is hidden or shown, one or two players, and the difficulty. Move to GO! and press Return to start. In a one-player game you are the white spy and the computer is the black spy. When a round ends you come back to this screen.

The fire button

A tap of fire does the obvious thing: pick up what you are standing on (a mound means dig), tap again to put it away. Hold fire and press a direction for the second action: bury what you are holding, open the Trapulator, or throw a snowball. In the Trapulator, left and right choose a tool and fire takes it out. The full table is in the developer notes.

A tip of the hat

Everything here stands on other people's work.

Antonio Prohías (1921–1998), a Cuban cartoonist who fled to the United States, created Spy vs. Spy: a wordless comic strip in MAD magazine, from 1961, in which a spy in white and a spy in black spend their lives trying to blow each other up. He drew it until 1987. Two hats, two long beaks, no dialogue, and it is still funny.

First Star Software turned the strip into the Spy vs. Spy computer games in the mid-1980s: Spy vs. Spy, The Island Caper and, in 1986–87, Arctic Antics, published in the US by Epyx. A split screen, a Trapulator full of cruel little gadgets, a body-heat bar and a good laugh when the other player walks into your saw hole: the design is so tight that it was still worth taking apart nearly forty years later.

antics exists because that game is a joy and its engine is worth understanding. It was built by studying the original program and measuring recordings of it running, then writing every rule again from scratch, so that a game made in 1987 keeps working on computers that did not exist yet. If you made the original, thank you.

About this project

  • From scratch. Original Rust source; not a fork of any engine or emulator, and no third-party game engine. SDL2 provides the window, keyboard and sound output.
  • Checked against the real thing. Behaviour is verified against recordings of the original running: item placement, body heat, traps, the intro, the selector, the round screen. The notes on how are in the developer docs.
  • Free software. GPL-3.0-or-later: use it, study it, change it and share it, as long as it stays free and comes with its source.
  • Source and issues: github.com/LyndonBlack/antics
